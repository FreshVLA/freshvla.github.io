The central idea

One action,
two timescales.

Watch a robot open a pot. Over several frames, the lid lifts clear. From one frame to the next, the gripper makes small adjustments at the handle.

Substage scale
Tracks how the task progresses across a longer span of video.
Execution scale
Captures local motion left over after accounting for that progress.

Learning actions from video

Part I Latent action pretraining

Where does the action happen?

Static backgrounds can help predict future frames. PAWKL uses a temporal high-pass prior to guide patch-level attention toward changes in the scene, visible in the lower row.

Visual robustness

With PAWKL, action-decoding error stays closer to its clean-trajectory level under all three visual perturbations. Under camera changes, the relative robustness score rises from 0.829 to 0.933 (1 denotes clean parity).

How does the action unfold?

The substage stream tracks progress over many frames, while the execution stream encodes local motion residuals, such as a grasp adjustment. Frequency regularization encourages this division across timescales.

Long-horizon gains

At 35 context steps, hierarchical continuous latent actions yield lower action-regression MSE than their single-scale counterpart (0.032 vs. 0.034). The same comparison raises LIBERO-Long success from 93.0% to 98.0%.

Part II Policy post-training

From video to control

During policy training, the frozen latent action encoder supplies targets for joint flow matching. At inference, the policy jointly generates latent and robot actions from current observations and the instruction, then executes the robot actions.
