Execution scale

Abstract

Learning actions from video

In a video of a robot stacking bowls, the drawer and table appear in every frame. The brief moment when the gripper touches a bowl tells us more about the action. Learning from video requires identifying those changes and connecting them over time.

FreshVLA uses a temporal high-pass prior to direct attention toward regions that change during manipulation. It learns continuous latent actions at two timescales. One tracks progress through a task, while the other captures local changes during execution. A frozen encoder then supplies latent targets for a flow-matching policy that learns to generate robot actions.

With 2.5 billion parameters, FreshVLA achieves 98.2% average success on LIBERO and 80.4% on zero-shot LIBERO-PLUS. Across four tasks on a physical robot, it improves average success by 7.5 percentage points over π0.5.
