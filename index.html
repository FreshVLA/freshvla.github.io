The central idea

One action,
two timescales.

Watch a robot open a pot. Over several frames, the lid lifts clear. From one frame to the next, the gripper makes small adjustments at the handle.

Substage scale
Tracks how the task progresses across a longer span of video.
Execution scale
Captures local motion left over after accounting for that progress.

Approach

Learning actions from video

FreshVLA learns temporally hierarchical continuous latent actions from action-free videos and transfers them to robot control.

Where does the action happen?

Background layouts and static appearance help predict future observations but are only weakly related to control. FreshVLA uses temporal high-pass responses to guide patch-level attention toward manipulation-relevant changes.

How does the action unfold?

A single transition scale can obscure the distinction between slowly evolving task progress and local execution dynamics. Cross-timescale sampling and frequency-domain regularization encourage the substage stream to capture long-horizon progress and the execution stream to capture local motion residuals.

From video to control

During policy training, the frozen latent action encoder provides targets for joint flow matching of latent and robot actions.
